---
name: paper-presentation
description: Turn a research paper and its verified figures into an explanatory academic presentation with readable slides and detailed speaker notes.
---

Develop an argument rather than a figure slideshow: research question, prior limitation, core idea, mechanism, evaluation, results and limits. Adapt to surveys, benchmarks and theory. Every slide needs one defensible message and source pages. Explain input/output, module responsibilities, variable meaning, assumptions and evidence. Never fabricate results or infer page numbers from figure numbers.

Keep slide text short enough to read; move detailed explanations into speaker notes instead of deleting them. Notes should explain why the step matters, how it operates, what evidence supports the statement and relevant caveats. Present numerical improvements with metric, baseline and conditions. Distinguish author claims from analysis. Cite physical PDF pages; mark unknown sources explicitly.

Use a consistent type hierarchy, generous margins, high contrast and varied layouts. Use original scientific figures and editable text/shapes. Localized SVGs may contain a raster original with editable labels: describe that honestly. Diagrams redrawn for teaching must be labeled as interpretations. Do not hide overflow by clipping meaningful text; split content or move detail into notes. Do not guess annotation coordinates without actually viewing the figure.

For figure annotations, provide a succinct claim title, an explanation of what the figure establishes, 2–5 specific module explanations, and substantive `speaker_notes`. Follow the caller's JSON schema. For narrative slides, follow the caller's outline schema and use only supplied source pages. A polished slide is not evidence of a scientifically correct explanation; preserve uncertainties in notes and final status.
